§01 · Abstract

A five-sentence summary of the research

Question. Can search intelligence signals — impressions, position, click-through rate, and content age — identify pages that are quietly losing visibility before the loss becomes severe?

Method. We frame refresh prioritization as a binary classification problem, comparing a transparent baseline rule against a Random Forest model trained on engineered search and content features.

Data. The models are trained on the FlyRank ML Internship dataset: over 79 million anonymized production search records stored in DuckDB, derived from Google Search Console signals across multiple content performance tables.

Result. The Random Forest model improves precision and recall over the baseline rule, and its feature importances offer a defensible, inspectable explanation for every prediction — not a black-box score.

Decision support. The output is a ranked, reason-coded worklist for content teams, not an autonomous action: every recommendation is designed to be reviewed by a human before a page is touched.

§02 · Introduction

Content decays quietly, long before anyone notices

Search visibility rarely disappears overnight. It erodes gradually — a slipping position here, a shrinking set of ranking queries there — until a page that once drove meaningful traffic is barely found at all. By the time a decline is obvious in a monthly report, the recoverable window has often already closed.

The problem

Content teams responsible for thousands of published pages cannot manually re-review each one on a recurring schedule. Manual triage does not scale past a few hundred URLs, and it tends to react to loss rather than anticipate it.

Why it matters

Organic search is frequently the largest, lowest-cost acquisition channel a content team owns. A page that quietly loses half its impressions is a quiet loss of pipeline, and refreshing it earlier is usually far cheaper than producing new content from zero.

Business impact

A ranked, explainable worklist turns an unbounded review problem into a short, prioritized queue — letting a small editorial team focus effort on the pages most likely to benefit from a refresh, rather than the pages that are simply newest or oldest.

Research motivation

This project asks whether a lightweight, explainable model — not a deep neural network, not an opaque score — can meaningfully outperform a simple rule, while still producing a reason a content editor can read, trust, and act on.

§04 · Methodology

From raw signals to a ranked, explainable prediction

Each stage was built to be inspectable on its own, so that the final ranking can be traced back to specific, human-readable signals.

Step 01

Data cleaning

Deduplicate page-level records, resolve conflicting rows across content performance tables, and drop pages with insufficient search history to produce a reliable signal.

Step 02

Feature engineering

Derive 12 features spanning content age, update recency, exposure (impressions, position, CTR), content depth (word count), and query diversity (visible queries, rare share, top-query share).

Step 03

Baseline rule

A transparent, non-learned rule: flag a page as declining if recent impressions have dropped relative to the prior period and the page has not been updated recently. This sets the floor the model must beat.

Step 04

Random Forest classifier

An ensemble of decision trees trained on the engineered features to predict declining vs. not-declining, chosen for its native feature-importance output and resistance to overfitting on tabular data.

Step 05

Group-aware validation

A GroupShuffleSplit keeps all rows for a given page in either the train or the test split, never both — preventing the same page's history from leaking across the split boundary.

Step 06

Leakage checking

Features that could only be known after the outcome (for example, future impression windows) were audited and excluded, so the model only sees information available at prediction time.

Step 07

Evaluation

Compare baseline vs. Random Forest on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1, alongside the base rate, so any lift is read against how hard the problem actually is.

Step 08

Recommendations

Convert per-page probabilities into High / Medium / Low priority tiers, each attached to a reason code drawn from the features that drove the prediction.

§06 · Recommendations

A ranked worklist, not an autonomous action

Every page receives a priority tier and at least one reason code, so an editor can see exactly why it was flagged.

High priority

Refresh this quarter

Pages combining a steep visibility decline with meaningful remaining exposure — the highest expected return on editorial time.

DECLINING_SIGNAL — impressions down >30% vs. prior period
HIGH_EXPOSURE — still among the page's top exposure tier
Medium priority

Add to the review queue

Pages showing early warning signs — stale content that is still visible, but without a confirmed decline yet.

STALE_VISIBLE — no update in 12+ months, still ranking
DECLINING_SIGNAL — mild downward trend, below the high-priority threshold
Low priority

Monitor only

Pages performing within their normal range — no action needed, revisit at the next scheduled review cycle.

LOW_PRIORITY — stable or improving trend across the window

§07 · Limitations & ethics

What this research does not claim

Read the results as decision support, not ground truth

  • Findings describe observed relationships in the training window, not proven causes of ranking changes.
  • The system is decision support only — it recommends a review, it does not publish or edit anything itself.
  • No causal claims are made about why any individual page's visibility changed.
  • This model does not attempt to predict or reverse-engineer Google's ranking algorithm.
  • All reported metrics come from anonymized, aggregated data with no client-identifying information.
  • Performance was validated with GroupShuffleSplit, but real-world drift over time still requires periodic re-evaluation.
Data minimization

Only the 12 engineered features are used for prediction; raw queries and URLs never enter the model.

Human in the loop

Every recommendation requires editorial sign-off before any content action is taken.

Honest reporting

Metrics are reported against the base rate, and placeholders are labeled clearly wherever exact figures are pending.
